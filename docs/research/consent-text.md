# DRAFT — pending IRB approval

**Nobody should be asked to consent to this text.** It is a draft written so the
screen exists and has a version string to record. The IRB-approved wording
replaces it, and changing `version:` above is what asks everyone again.

## What the screen says

**Help work out whether this actually helps**

This app keeps a record of what you predicted and what happened. With your
permission, a stripped-down copy of that record can be used to study whether
keeping it changes anything.

**What would be shared**

Numbers and choices: how sure you were, whether it came true, how surprised you
were, when things happened, which questionnaire scores were recorded.

**What would never be shared**

Anything you wrote. Not the situation, not what you expected, not what actually
happened, not your journal, not the words you used for your rules, not anything
you told your clinician. Your name and email are not in this app's records at
all, and they are not in the study's either.

**How you are identified**

You are not. Each export replaces your account with a scrambled code that
cannot be traced back and is different every time. Every date is moved by a
random number of days, the same shift for all of yours — so how far apart
things happened stays exactly right, and what day they happened does not.

**It is up to you, and you can change your mind**

This is off unless you turn it on. Your clinician is not told either way, and
cannot see this screen or your answer. Turning it off stops your record being
included in anything from that moment on. Exports already made cannot be
recalled, because there is no way to find you in them — that is the same
protection working in the other direction.

**It changes nothing about your care**

Not what you see in the app, not what your clinician sees, not what happens in a
session.
