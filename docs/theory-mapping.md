## 1. Vocabulary the app should use verbatim

The corpus is unusually consistent about its terms, and a client who later reads the books or sits with a clinician trained on the paper will meet the same words.

**Prediction, and why it is not a guess.** UCM §2.1: "A prediction here is not a guess. A guess is conscious, held loosely, and checked against experience. A prediction is none of these: it is built below awareness, delivered as fact, and *is* the experience." CG restates it as a table. The trade-book synonym is **forecast** (WtD Ch2: "Stress is a prediction your machine makes about what the world is about to do to you."). WtD's client-facing labels can go on screen as written: "**This is a prediction, not a fact**" (Ch2), "**The dread is a bet, not a verdict**" (Ch2), "**A feeling is a forecast wearing a body**" and "**A feeling is data, not a verdict**" (Ch6), "**The machine updates on experience, not on argument**" (Ch10).

**Prior.** CG: "A prior is a standing prediction … learned where the person actually lived, usually early, usually without words." UCM §2.2 adds the discipline the app must keep: "first ask whether the prior is actually miscalibrated." A **calibrated prior** is "an accurate read of a punishing present."

**Prediction error, and surprise as its readout.** UCM §2.1: prediction error "is the only genuinely new information in the system." RN-Surprise: "**Surprise is prediction error arriving in consciousness.**" UCM §6.2: "if nothing surprised the client, nothing changed."

**Precision, the dimmer.** UCM §2.2: the brain "weights each signal by its estimated reliability — its **precision** — and updates more in response to signals it trusts"; "precision is the dimmer on every incoming signal"; and the consequence: "**The corrective signal is usually not missing — it is arriving and being weighted at zero.**" MOD calls the relationship side of this "the valve."

**Dial versus gauge.** UCM §4.2: "a dial is set; a gauge is read." R&U-Gauges: "The gauges are read, never turned." A second use of "dial" appears in UCM §8, the "ten risers" that sort predictions "by **domain** — what they are about, and therefore which rooms activate them." Reserve "dial" for precision and keep the domain-dials clinician-side.

**The two gauges.** R&U Ch9: the status gauge "reads rank, not welcome. It asks one question. *Am I above or below?*"; the belonging gauge "tracks **being wanted** — being liked, being chosen, being someone people are glad to have around. It asks a completely different question: *do they want me here?*" UCM §4.2 uses the research names, **mattering** "tracked by the *sociometer*" and **rank** "tracked by a *hierometer* function"; R&U-Bridge maps them one to one. UCM §4.2 further splits mattering into **belonging** ("admission; being in, having a place at the table") and **mattering proper** ("significance; being needed by particular people").

**The furnace and its five moves.** UCM §2.3: "There are only two ways to kill a prediction error: update the belief, or create a world in which it is true." The moves are **Provoke, Avoid, Filter, Reinterpret, Withdraw effort**; provoke and withdraw effort "manufacture a real confirming consequence," avoid "*prevents the test*," and filter and reinterpret are "the Part 2.2 dimmer working defensively." WtD Ch10's lay trio: "It dodges. It discounts. It stages." **Prepayment** is explicitly not a furnace move: "he pays the predicted harm in advance," having learned "that calm is not evidence" (UCM §2.3; "Escape is prepayment with compounding interest," P-Panic).

**The press secretary.** UCM §2.4: the explaining voice "has no sightline into its own causes." Rule four is the app's most usable sentence: "the size of the error is proportional to the confidence of the belief it just contradicted."

**Protocol and trade-book terms.** **Armor** and **the kit** are safety behaviors ("water, pills, phone, the aisle seat, the exit map, the safe person," P-Panic Phase 1); the **deadband** is "a range of bodily weather that passes unexamined" (P-Panic Phase 3); the **summons** is deliberate interoceptive exposure; a **reading** is the written result. R&U's **the wall** is the sealed man and **the code** is "a price list you were handed" (Ch6). WtD's three moves: "**The catch** — seeing an automatic thing in the act instead of being carried by it. **The test** — writing down what you predict and taking it somewhere reality can answer. **The practice** — one small thing, daily, with no finish line" (front matter). The app's loop is his second move.

## 2. The floors, and which ones the loop can touch

UCM §3.1, top down: (1) **Narrative** — "the binding story, the single 'you' stretched across time"; (2) **Metacognition** — "beliefs about your own mind: whether thoughts are facts, whether feelings are dangerous, whether change is possible"; (3) **Propositional beliefs** — "the explicit convictions about self, others, world, future, and value"; (4) **Relational patterns** — "the self-in-relation: what you expect from closeness, the template laid down by early attachment"; (5) **Scripts and behavior** — "learned sequences that run a familiar situation to its known end before thought arrives"; (6) **Procedure and emotion** — "stored action and sensory traces with built emotional responses fused to them"; (7) **Body and interoception** — "the felt boundary of the self and the constant inner read of the body's state"; (8) **The cultural substrate** — "the language, categories, and norms absorbed before they could be weighed; not one belief among others but the medium the structure is built in."

Two honesty notes the app inherits: floor 4 "straddles the building," with "a sayable component that lives near floor 3" and "a procedural-emotional component … that runs at floor-6 depth" (UCM §3.1; F4); and the floors are "a teaching device, not anatomy," with the vertical ordering "this model's own proposal, not a finding" (UCM §3.1, §3.6).

What the loop reaches. His crosswalk lands behavioral experiments on **floors 3 and 5** (MOD, CBT table) and exposure-as-expectancy-violation on **floors 5–6** (UCM §6.3; F5: "which floor hosts the block, which floor takes the write"). So the loop's native ground is the sayable verdict (3) and the avoidance or safety-behavior sequence the experiment blocks (5), with a write reaching 6 when the test is run live and disconfirms. The interoceptive track reaches **floor 7** through "interoceptive exposure for the panic loop" and "attention training for the numbness" (F7). Floor 2 is reachable indirectly, because both anxiety protocols cost-account a floor-2 "policy" (body-scanning; the inward camera and the post-mortem) before experiments begin (P-Panic Phase 3; P-SA Phase 3).

What it cannot reach, in his words. **Floor 4's deep copy** updates only through "the relationship itself behaving off-template while the template is live" (F4); "a person over time is the only evidence that bears on what closeness costs" (UCM §5). **Floor 6 reconsolidation** needs the trace "live, felt in the room" and inside the window of tolerance (F6); P-PTSD's memory work is "co-run under supervision or referred until licensure and trauma-specific training." **Floor 1** re-authoring comes last in every protocol ("Now floor 3 writes, because the ledger exists for the sentences to describe … Floor 1 re-authors," P-Panic Phase 7), so the ledger feeds it but the app should not attempt it. **Floor 8** is "assessed, never assumed," and where the environment is the pathogen "no upper-floor tool reaches it" (UCM §6.1). Treat 1, 4-deep, 6, and 8 as out of scope and say so in the clinician view.

## 3. Interoception (Floor 7) mapped onto the loop

**What a body signal is.** F7: "**Interoception**: the running prediction of the body's own condition — arousal, energy, hunger, pain — which, like all perception, is a model corrected by data, not a gauge wired to the flesh." WtD Ch6 for the lay reader: "The body supplies the intensity. *The machine assigns the meaning*." Two constraints follow. Floor 7 is "the alarm hardware: domain-general arousal, contentless, **one alarm line**," and "The body alone cannot tell you which dial fired … the interview asks *which room were you in*" (F7). And "floor-7 errors masquerade as beliefs downstream — the racing heart *read as* dying produces a floor-3 catastrophe report, but the load is the interoceptive prior, and arguing the report leaves the misreading intact" (F7).

**What must be logged for the prior to update.** WtD Ch10: "**The forecast has to be firing — live, felt in the body — at the exact moment reality proves it wrong.**" P-Panic explains why the ordinary record never converts: "escape keeps filing each survival under *rescued* instead of *false alarm*." So the before-record needs the channel and its verdict ("heart → dying; breath → suffocating; head → stroke, madness, losing control; legs and vision → collapsing in front of everyone … the verdict is the forecast, verbatim," P-Panic Phase 1); the during-record needs whether it was "run **past the peak** rather than to the first flinch, with no armor on the table"; and the after-record needs what the survival was credited to, because the rule is "arrive without the kit … so the credit for every survival lands on the body, not the bottle" (P-Panic Phase 4–5). A survival logged with armor present is, on his account, not a disconfirmation.

**Panic and PTSD priors.** P-Panic: "**Panic is a false alarm about the alarm** … sensation → render (*heart attack, now*) → alarm floods the body → more sensation → certainty." He tells the client in advance that "**anxiety rises first**," and that "Breathing techniques deployed *during* attacks are armor in disguise." PTSD's body reading differs in kind: hypervigilance is "the dial's precision cranked planet-wide, the deadband at zero everywhere"; the body is re-entered "as experiments, not rescue rituals"; dissociation "is not a contraindication; it is a pacing instruction," with grounding trained "to fluency *before* any trauma content"; and "outside the window, nothing files" (P-PTSD Phases 0, 3, 5, 6; F6: "floor 6 only updates while it's firing, and it can't update while it's flooding").

**Why the track fits veterans, in his words.** R&U Ch6: "**Not being calm and collected was a sign of incompetence.**" R&U Ch4 gives the alexithymic presentation: "He has a fast heart rate and a tight jaw and an urge to leave the room, and no name for any of it, and no map from the sensation to the word." F7's specimen: "A man reports genuine calm while his knuckles are white on the armrest. He isn't lying — the alarm is ringing on a floor his awareness doesn't visit." F7's prescription: "render the signals back into awareness, name them — building the vocabulary floor 7 was never taught." So the track has two jobs needing separate fields: pair sensation with the failed catastrophe for the panic-shaped client; pair sensation with a word and with what followed for the numb one.

**Cues and scales he already uses.** "anxiety 0–10, 'how badly it will show' 0–10, the machine's exact prediction in words" (P-SA Phase 4); confidence as a percent (UCM §6.3; "how sure the machine was, zero to a hundred," WtD Ch10); his own thought record at 0–100 belief and 0–100 shame (R&U Ch16); panic baselines "attack log (frequency, duration, peak intensity), PDSS, the avoidance map's item count, the armor inventory, scans per day, caffeine load" (P-Panic Phase 1); the floor-7 vocabulary "tight, heavy, buzzing" (F7); and the timing rule, "run the check when the feeling is at an eleven and you'll lose … wait out the crest … and *then* audit" (WtD Ch6).

**Proposed fields.** `body_state_before`: `channel` (heart / breath / head / unreality / stomach / muscle / numb — P-Panic Phase 4 plus F7's numbness case); `intensity_0_10` (P-SA); `words` (seeded with "tight, heavy, buzzing," F7); `verdict` (what the body means right now, verbatim, P-Panic Phase 1); `verdict_confidence_0_100` (UCM §6.3; WtD Ch10); `room` ("which room were you in," F7); `kit_present` (armor items, P-Panic Phase 1); `substances_last_12h` (P-Panic Phase 0 substances audit; P-SA "experiments run sober or they don't count"); `sleep_last_night` (WtD Ch11: "Every skill in this book gets harder on six hours"); `scan_triggered` ("checking the heart raises the heart," P-Panic).

`body_state_after`: `peak_intensity_0_10` and `ran_past_peak` (P-Panic Phase 4); `time_to_crest_min` (WtD Ch6, Ch8); `verdict_arrived` (yes / partly / no — the panic "due date"); `credited_to` (body / kit item / safe person / technique / luck — the accounting P-Panic Phase 5 exists to fix); `kit_used` (including breathing during the episode); `felt_vs_observed` (optional external check such as "what did your hands do," from F7's white-knuckle specimen and P-SA's video-feedback logic); `window_status` (present versus "unable to say what actually happened," which WtD Ch10 says means "it was too big"); `word_now` (the after-name, so the numb client's vocabulary accumulates). The clinician view should count "survivals without kit" separately; it is the only column that converts *rescued* into *false alarm*.

One schema-level boundary: the summons requires "appropriate medical workup … **complete, and then closed**" and "Interoceptive clearance … cleared with the physician where any real condition exists" (P-Panic Phase 0). A `clearance_on_file` gate, without which summons exercises are not offered, is the faithful implementation.

## 4. Precision and surprise, translated into app rules

**Update versus explained away.** UCM §2.2: "When a prior and incoming evidence disagree, the outcome lands nearer whichever is narrower and more confident. The narrower bet wins, whether or not it is correct." A high-confidence miss is therefore the loudest signal available (UCM §2.4 rule four). But RN-Surprise adds: "**Surprise marks registration, not update.** A client can be visibly surprised and have it excused within thirty seconds — *huh, weird, anyway* … **The clinically interesting window is the half-minute after the surprise.**" And: "**Two readouts: surprise = error landing. The swerve = error being killed.**"

**Conditions he names for failure to update.** The prediction was not live ("a contradiction with no live prediction is just news," WtD Ch10; "a summons the alarm ignores teaches nothing," P-Panic). The test flooded ("past the window of tolerance the valve slams shut," F6; "the machine files *survived by luck* instead of *forecast missed*," WtD Ch10). Armor present ("The disconfirmation gets credited to the safety behavior," MOD). The furnace ran the test ("the client runs the test but provokes, filters, or withdraws effort so it confirms the fear," MOD). Reinterpretation ("*they were only being nice*," UCM §2.3; "it was safe because we were in your office," P-Panic). The record was not written first ("Memory silently rewrites an unrecorded prediction to match the outcome," UCM §6.3; "Reconstruction is the machine writing the answer key after the test," WtD Ch10). The source is discounted (the client's own evidence outweighs the therapist's identical point, MOD on MI; reassurance "gets filed under paid politeness," P-SA). And tonic error, which never shows up as surprise: "Tonic error presents as mood, not as surprise" (RN-Surprise).

**Rules.** Make the mismatch loud only when confidence was high and the outcome missed the *stated* verdict, and make it loud in his way, the number read back against the record ("Nothing embarrasses a forecast like its own confidence, read back a month later next to its record," WtD Ch10). Do not make it loud when the kit was present or the client could not say what happened; route those to the clinician. Capture the half-minute: after the outcome and surprise rating, ask one dated question, "what did you tell yourself about why it went that way," and store circumstance-attributions as a separate `reinterpretation` record that cannot edit the outcome — P-SA does exactly this ("'they didn't notice this time' gets written down and dated, so its record can be checked"). A surprise rating should capture three things he separates: whether the outcome violated the stated prediction (registration), whether the client was present for it (window), and whether the bet "missed sideways," which UCM §6.3 calls "not a failed experiment but a floor reading" and which should be tagged for the clinician rather than scored. The outcome field needs a partial option, because "half the time reality confirms the small annoyance and still disconfirms the catastrophe" (WtD Ch10).

## 5. The rupture-and-repair thesis

His claim, UCM §5: "**Rupture-and-repair is therefore not a threat to the alliance but the mechanism by which a paid relationship generates un-manufactured evidence** (Eubanks, Muran, & Safran, 2018); a therapy that never ruptures has never put the therapist's regard on the line, and a regard never at risk teaches a floor-four prior nothing." The set-up is his own objection: the therapist's regard "is paid for, time-bounded, and professionally obliged," so "the informative moments are the ones where the regard could genuinely drop and does not." The deliberate version is "*Rung zero: the room*" — disclosing "the resentment toward therapy, the thing he is ashamed to have needed, the *I almost didn't come back*," which is "rupture-and-repair run deliberately rather than waited for" (UCM §6.3). WtD Ch10 closes the exit: "*It doesn't count* is not an insight about therapy. It's the bodyguard, challenging the evidence on a technicality."

A faithful prep/debrief captures, before: the prediction about the therapist's regard in the client's words, a confidence number, and the step-6 pre-commitment ("*how many times would this have to happen before you'd revise the rule?*"). After: whether the regard was "free to drop and did not" as the client experienced it; whether a swerve happened ("the topic change, the joke at the wrong moment, the 'yes, but,' the sudden fog," RN-Surprise); the fee-exit logged as a reinterpretation; and R&U Ch16's fidelity check, asked in those words: "Then sit still. Don't explain it. Don't take it back. Don't wrap it in a joke thirty seconds later — which is the most common escape hatch and it *destroys the rep*." Two things the flow must not do. It must not treat the app's responses as evidence: "**Programmed warmth is not wanting** … A machine can teach you the reroute. It cannot be the evidence" (R&U Ch16). And it must not manufacture ruptures; the therapist's part is "declining the role the prior has assigned, staying warm where the script called for retreat" (UCM §6.5), so the clinician prep should prompt the clinician to record "the therapist's own reaction" as data (UCM §6.5) rather than act on it.

## 6. Prior clustering: a starter taxonomy in his words

UCM §4.1: "the priors about connection and standing are held more tightly and defended harder than any others." UCM §6.1 question five: "Ask where the injury weights — toward being unwanted (the inclusion input) or being low (the rank input)." R&U's title condition is the cluster the practice exists for — "**Respected and unwanted** … the man everybody counts on and nobody knows" (Ch9) — with its mechanism, "he reads the drop as a status shortfall, because status is the only instrument anyone ever taught him to read" (Ch10), and its two furnace styles, "**Above them** — dominance … **Apart from them** — invulnerability" (Ch9).

Under **mattering / belonging (sociometer)**: "*If you show this, it will cost you*" (R&U Ch15; R&U-Bridge's "buried forecast"); "*if I show weakness, they withdraw*" (UCM §6.3); "*People only want you when you're useful*" (RN-Guess) with "**Being useful is not being wanted**" (R&U Ch9); "*being wanted is a thing you win*" (R&U Ch10, the wired-together variant, tell: "unearned warmth bounces off him"); "*if I say no, something between us breaks … and it won't come back*" (WtD Ch10, fawn); "*If people really knew me, they'd leave*" (RN-Guess); and, risk-gated, "*They'd be better off without me*" (UCM §4.2, "a sociometer reading gone negative").

Under **rank (hierometer)**: "*they'll see it and judge*" (FR; P-SA); "*If I speak up in that meeting, they'll think less of me*," "*If I ask for help, I'll look like I couldn't handle it*," "*If I admit I haven't read it … they'll think less of me*" (WtD Ch10); "*lose the point, lose the room*" (WtD Ch7); "my value *is* my rank" (UCM §4.2); "*Not being calm and collected was a sign of incompetence*" (R&U Ch6).

Under **body / threat (floor 7, prepayment)**: "*this sensation = catastrophe now*," "*this symptom is the disease*," "*hurt = harm; my body is fragile*," "*unresolved = unsafe*" (FR table); "*If I rest, everything falls apart*," "*If I stop checking, that's exactly when it falls apart*" (WtD Ch10); "calm is not evidence" (UCM §2.3).

Under **effort / futility**: "*effort won't pay; reward won't arrive*" (FR); "*What's the point. It won't work anyway*" and "*being noticed is being harmed*" (WtD Ch5, Ch4, freeze).

Under **the self-story**: "*I am not enough, and it must not show*" (FR); "*I am what happened*" (P-PTSD, routed, not app-run); "*I'm the screwup of the family*" (RN-Guess).

Two design notes. "Nobody is a type. The map's job is to help you find your own defaults, not to hand you a new verdict to wear" (WtD Ch4): show clusters as the forecast the entries keep landing on, never a diagnosis. And the four-F frame (WtD Ch4) maps onto WtD Ch10's opening experiments but is labeled "clinical wisdom rather than settled science," so it belongs in the client on-ramp, not the clinician formulation.

## 7. Mapping features onto evidence-based technique

**The loop is his six-step behavioral experiment** (UCM §6.3): name the prediction; make it falsifiable and rate confidence; "Write it down, before"; run the smallest real test; compare out loud; ask "how many times would this have to happen before you'd revise the rule?" The app has steps 1–3 and 5. It lacks step 6, which "turns one result into a standing experiment and pre-commits the client to updating." Add it. His design citation is Craske et al. (2014), named in UCM §6.3 and pinned in all three protocols: expectancy violation over habituation, block safety behaviors, vary context, expect relapse because "inhibitory learning does *not* erase the original fear prior." He caps the theory claim: "expectancy-violation is consistent with predictive processing, not uniquely predicted by it."

**Calibration over time is his ledger** — "the written ledger holds them" (P-Panic Phase 4), "the ledger of forecasts-versus-readings" (P-SA Phase 7), "the perimeter ledger" (P-PTSD), WtD Ch10's "list of wrong forecasts in your own handwriting," and R&U-Bridge's "prediction ledger." The "14× predicted, 2× happened" display is faithful, with his caveat that the client's sentences about it come last, "never the therapist's testimony" (P-SA Phase 7).

**Thought records.** Endorsed from his own use as "the side door … you work evidence, not feelings, the whole way through" (R&U Ch16). MOD's failure row is the app's flag: "'I know it's not true and I still feel it.' Accurate insight that changes nothing means a floor-3 tool aimed at a floor-6/7 prior."

**Interoceptive exposure.** P-Panic Phase 4 names "the Barlow/Craske panic-control lineage, interoceptive exposure, and the safety-behavior literature (Salkovskis)" and runs each exercise "as forecast experiments."

**Exposure hierarchy, corrected.** He keeps the ladder for disclosure targets ("rank five people by how safe they feel; start at the safest, not the most important," UCM §6.3; WtD Ch11) and rejects it for dose: "varied in order, context, and dose rather than climbed like a ladder" (P-Panic, P-SA Phase 4). A hierarchy feature should order recipients, not sensations.

**Two-condition experiments, defusion, debrief, monitoring.** "armor on versus armor off, with a written forecast *before* each" (P-SA Phase 4) is a paired-trial template. "*I'm having the thought that ______*" (WtD Ch5) is ACT defusion, with MOD's placement rule: "**ACT is the tool for the true or calibrated prior; CBT for the false one.**" The debrief screen is P-SA Phase 3 nearly verbatim: "one structured reading, same night or next morning — *what did I predict, what did I actually observe, what surprised me* — written, two minutes, done." And UCM §6.7 asks for "a validated symptom measure and a brief alliance measure session over session" (Lambert, 2010); the protocols name PDSS, LSAS, PHQ-9, PCL-5, DES-II.

## 8. Where the loop is not faithful, and what a skeptical reviewer will attack

**Self-report versus the floor.** DA: the floor "is read from signs and patterns the client is the last to see, not asked for by self-report." He defends the prediction field in UCM §6.3 — the protocol asks "for a forward bet about an external event, which the world grades, not the storyteller" — but that does not cover in-app floor-locating, nor the *outcome* field: "the felt version is the render under review" (P-SA Phase 5) and "Forecasts about other minds are still forecasts, and they're the ones your machine checks least" (WtD Ch6). Hold outcomes to "what they actually said and did" (WtD Ch6) and flag mind-read outcomes.

**A guess is not a prediction.** His own sharpest distinction (UCM §2.1) cuts against step one: the typed prediction is the rider's guess about the elephant's forecast. He supplies the remedy (the sideways miss is "a floor reading"), but a reviewer will ask how often the stated bet was the operative one.

**The chart is rider food.** WtD Ch10: "the rider learns from information. The elephant learns from outcomes." He supports reading confidence against the record, but the write happens in the half-minute after the live miss, not in a summary. Say so: the display motivates and pre-commits; it does not itself update the prior.

**The app can become the furnace and the wall.** UCM §6.3: "a structured program of self-experiments — logged, measured, optimized — is *itself* another exciting build … Keep it to an index card; if the client turns it into a system, that is not diligence, it is the finding." R&U Ch16: "if therapy becomes another scoreboard — streaks, metrics, optimization — the wall has reasserted in the shape of the cure." WtD Ch12: "There are no streaks here." Every ordinary retention mechanic is a named failure mode, and the flight-wired client is the one it captures. He also warns the app can be "a painkiller for the exact signal that was supposed to send you to the dentist," so it needs his audit: "If your human count is falling while your app count climbs, that isn't therapy" (R&U Ch16).

**Reinterpretation at the point of entry.** The outcome box is where "*they were only being nice*" gets typed. Unless reinterpretation is a separate dated record (section 4), the app is an instrument for the filter and reinterpret moves.

**The gates.** Three protocol gates precede any experiment and none is native to the loop: acute risk ("The mattering/burdensomeness lens *understands* suicidal desire; it does not replace risk management," DA); the reversed dial ("psychosis, mania … Do **not** try to loosen priors or generate prediction error here; it can destabilize," DA); and the calibrated prior ("Running a disclosure experiment into an environment that will confirm the fear is not therapy; it hands the prior fresh evidence with the therapist's signature on it," UCM §6.3), including screening for "coercive control or intimate-partner violence." R&U Ch16 adds the rooms where the filter "is still doing the exact job it was built for" — "a cell block … a crew, in a unit" — and renames the task: "Not softening — sorting." A veterans app will meet that client; it needs a "which room" gate and a clinician-owned safe-to-test flag per prior, or the counter will score correct reads of hostile rooms as failures to update.

**Timing and activation.** "nobody mentalizes mid-activation" (F7); the check "bounces off" a feeling "that's still landing" (WtD Ch6). Prompts during the feared situation ask for a reading he says cannot be taken. Schedule the after-entry past the crest and the before-entry earlier than the doorway.

**Grounding as armor.** "Breathing techniques deployed *during* attacks are armor in disguise" (P-Panic Phase 5), yet "Grounding to fluency" is the PTSD platform (P-PTSD Phase 3). An in-app grounding feature is armor in one protocol and treatment in another; the app has to know which it is serving.

**Grief before tools.** R&U Ch8: "**the bill has to land** … Grief is not the emotion you have about the update. Grief is the update." The loop has no place for this, and the sealed client is the one he says will skip it into "action and analysis."

**Vocabulary drift.** "Dial" is precision in UCM §2.2 and a domain-riser in §8; a gauge is read, never set. A screen that says "turn the dial down on your rejection prediction" breaks the distinction he was most deliberate about.

**What the reviewer will say, in his own words.** The mechanism is "a testable proposal rather than an established finding" (UCM abstract); "the precision mathematics is far better grounded for low-level perception than for the upper floors this model leans on" (UCM §7); the floors are "a teaching device, not anatomy"; the risers "were made, not found; the green cells are a composite; no reliability data exist for any of it" (UCM §8); every protocol is "made, not found" with "no outcome data of its own"; the appendices "are not instruments." He also names the unfalsifiability charge against himself: "if you nod, the book is right; if you tighten, the book is more right. An argument built that way cannot lose, and an argument that cannot lose has stopped being an argument" (R&U Ch15). A reviewer will apply that to "theory surfaced at the moment of mismatch": if every miss is the furnace and every hit is calibration, the app cannot be wrong.
