# Floor 3 — Propositional Beliefs

Floor notes series, 12 August 2026. Companions: §3.1, §3.4, §3.5, the floor-presentations bank.

---

## The essence: the sayable convictions

The paper's definition: "the explicit convictions about self, others, world, future, and value."
Floor 3 is the floor made of sentences — beliefs a person can state, defend, and debate. It is
the most legible floor, the one the intake form reaches, and the home turf of the entire
cognitive tradition. Its predictions run in propositional format: *people only want you when
you're useful; the world doesn't forgive mistakes; needing help is weakness.* Because the format
is language, the floor is glass: words genuinely reach it, and a good conversation really can
rearrange it — which is both its clinical value and its trap.

## Against its neighbors

- **Versus floor 1:** a proposition versus a plot. Floor 3's entries can be flipped one at a
  time; floor 1 metabolizes flipped entries back into the story.
- **Versus floor 2:** beliefs about the world and self versus beliefs about the machinery.
- **Versus floors 4/6 — the crucial one:** the same content routinely exists in **two copies**:
  a sayable floor-3 copy and a concrete copy below. *"If people really knew me, they'd leave"*
  can be held as a debatable sentence AND as a floor-6 fused trace that fires in the body when
  intimacy approaches. The copies dissociate: the sentence can be corrected while the trace
  runs on. This is the thermometer patient of §2.3, and it generates the most important
  diagnostic in the model: **winning the argument and losing the temperature means the
  load-bearing copy is below the verbal floors.** Floor-3 work that fails is never wasted if
  read as a probe.

## How it gets written

By instruction and inference: family mottos said out loud, conclusions drawn from events and
never re-examined, borrowed convictions from the water (floor 8) adopted as personal findings.
Some floor-3 entries are honest summaries of concrete floors below ("I say I expect people to
leave because, somewhere, leaving is what my body predicts"); others are freestanding — actually
learned as sentences — and those are the ones cognitive work moves fast.

## What reaches it — and why it works

§3.4 assigns **CBT and REBT**. The mechanism is the model's cheapest lever: examine the content,
then engineer a real error to test it — the behavioral experiment, which converts a debate into
a reading. When the load is actually here, this works quickly and durably, which is why the
cognitive tradition earned its evidence base. Two working rules from the engine:

1. **Always pair the content move with a real test.** Revising the sentence without arranging a
   reading leaves the update on paper; §2.3's furnace can manufacture opposite evidence faster
   than the worksheet can supply corrections.
2. **Let failure inform.** Genuine update that cools the domain → the load was here; every point
   conceded and nothing changes → the load is below, and the treatment plan should move floors
   rather than sharpen arguments.

## From your chair

The floor-3 tell is fluency: the client can argue both sides of their own belief, cite
counter-evidence, even laugh about it — *and the fluency is the tell that debate is not the
treatment* when the pattern persists anyway. Floor-3-proper presentations are stated flatly,
like facts about the weather, with curated evidence attached.

The five presentations (composites):

1. "I'm lazy." Offered as a neutral fact, like a blood type — with a lifetime of curated
   evidence attached.
2. "People only want you when you're useful."
3. "You get one chance in life. The world doesn't forgive mistakes."
4. "Needing help is weakness. I handle things myself." Presents at intake while apologizing for
   being at intake.
5. "If people really knew me, they'd leave." Explicit, sayable, argued fluently — the fluency
   is the tell.

## Ten in the wild

All composites. The floor-3 tell in each: the conviction is *sayable* — stated as fact, often
with curated evidence — and the person can usually debate it fluently.

1. "Nobody does anything for free." Stated like thermodynamics; every kindness audited for the
   invoice.
2. A woman keeps forty thousand dollars in checking and won't invest: "money disappears when
   you're not looking at it" — a Depression-era grandmother's sentence, held as a market fact.
3. A man turns down every promotion: "managers are the enemy. I'm not becoming one."
4. "There are two kinds of people — those who leave and those who get left. I'm the second
   kind." Recited fluently, debated fluently, unchanged.
5. "If it's not perfect, it's garbage." A binary quality standard applied to work, dinner
   parties, and herself.
6. A man believes asking a question in a meeting reveals ignorance permanently: "you get one
   first impression, forever." Fourteen years without a question asked.
7. "All conflict destroys relationships" — so a marriage with no open disagreement in a decade,
   and the intimacy of a stranger's handshake.
8. "My needs are extra. Other people have real problems." Self-triage as a stated policy.
9. "Trust is earned in years and lost in seconds." Runs his relationships like a probation
   office and calls it prudence.
10. "I'm just not a math person." Installed at twelve by one teacher's sentence; still routing
    career decisions at forty.

## In the machinery

Floor 3 is where the furnace's *filter and reinterpret* moves do their bookkeeping — the
sentences that re-describe disconfirming events until they stop counting ("they were only being
nice") are drafted here in the prior's defense. And the wall thermometer lives here: the
accurate, agreed-upon reading of the room that the third furnace patient holds while staying
cold. Floor 3's honest role in deep work is often *preparation and consolidation* — the floor
where the client understands what the experiential work will do, and the floor where a floor-6
rewrite gets put into words afterward so the narrative desk can file it.
