# Floor 4 — Relational Patterns

Floor notes series, 12 August 2026. Companions: §3.1 (including the straddle honesty note),
§3.4, §3.5, §2.3 (the furnace shares a wall), the dial doctrine note, and the risers image.

---

## The essence: the self-in-relation

The paper's definition: "the self-in-relation — what you expect from closeness, the template
laid down by early attachment." Floor 4 holds predictions whose *subject is other people in
relation to me*: what happens when I let someone matter, what closeness costs, who stays, what
power does when it notices me. The internal working model, in Bowlby's sense — and the paper's
§2.3 already showed what makes these predictions special: they are the ones most able to
*manufacture their own confirmation*, because their subject can be acted upon. A belief about
gravity can only be tested; a belief about abandonment can be provoked.

## The straddle — floor 4's defining honesty note

§3.1 says it plainly: **floor 4 straddles the building.** Relational material has a sayable
component that lives near floor 3 — *"I expect people to leave"* — and a procedural-emotional
component, the working model proper, that runs at floor-6 depth. Floor 4 is best held as a
*domain* with two storage formats: which is exactly what the risers image generalized (every
dial is a vertical column through the building; floor 4 was the case the paper conceded first).
Clinical consequence: attachment work usually has to be experiential, and the questionnaire
reaches the copy that isn't running the pattern.

## Against its neighbors

- **Versus floor 3:** the sayable copy versus the operating copy — see the straddle.
- **Versus floor 5:** a relational *template* versus a behavioral *sequence*. The template
  selects and interprets ("she's pulling away"); the script executes ("so I check the phone").
  Template upstream, script downstream — the furnace runs them in series.
- **Versus the dials:** the ten dials are floor-4 content sorted by kind of room. The dial
  doctrine's question — *which dial did this room just wire to?* — is floor-4 formulation with
  a domain index on it.

## How it gets written

In the original rooms, by the thousands of trials nobody counted: bedtime, comfort-when-hurt,
who stayed, what happened after voices rose, what needing cost. High stakes, high repetition,
mostly pre-verbal — which is why the deep copy is concrete. Each dial was set in the childhood
version of the room it now governs (the panel note, §4): the intimate template in the family's
inner rooms, the authority template under whoever held power and how they used it.

## What reaches it — and why it works

§3.4 assigns **psychodynamic, interpersonal, and attachment-based therapies**, "where the
transference *is* the assessment." The mechanism has two halves:

1. **Make the template legible.** The pattern reproduces indifferent to circumstance, so it
   will reproduce with you — and the room becomes the one place the template can be observed
   running rather than reported on.
2. **Supply relational prediction error.** The corrective experience is the relationship
   itself behaving off-template while the template is live: the rupture survived, the anger met
   without retaliation, the dependency not punished. This is §2.3's practice rule in its home
   domain — and the reason the furnace must be interrupted *in the room*: intellectual insight
   without the live relational error is inert.

The Part 5 argument lands here with full force: a setting worn on the upper back is adjusted
only from behind, by someone allowed to stand steadily in the one place the client cannot
watch. Floor 4 is the floor that sentence was written for.

## From your chair

The §3.5 signs that point here: the every-time pattern (across partners, jobs, decades,
indifferent to circumstance); it shows up *with you*; and your own pull — the drowsiness, the
sudden carefulness, the urge to rescue that arrives with one client and no other is their
template registering on your instruments (Part 6.5).

The five presentations (composites):

1. "I keep ending up with the same person in different bodies."
2. Merge-then-panic: moved in by week six, suffocating by month three — closeness has two
   speeds and no middle.
3. The tester: picks the fight to find out whether this one stays. (The furnace's flagship
   case — floor 4 and §2.3 share a wall.)
4. Cannot be alone, cannot be close: a serial-relationship history presented as bad luck.
5. The template running live in the room: reads the rescheduled session as rejection — walks in
   the door mid-transference.

## Ten in the wild

All composites. The floor-4 tell in each: the prediction is about *people in relation to me*,
it reproduces across relationships indifferent to circumstance, and it can recruit its own
confirmation.

1. A woman interviews every new friendship for the exit: logs the first canceled plan, starts
   withdrawing "before it gets one-sided" — and friendships keep proving one-sided.
2. A man makes himself indispensable — the taxes, the cars, the crises — because being needed
   is the only tenure he trusts. His partner's growing competence reads as notice given.
3. A woman dates unavailable men serially; the one available man "had no chemistry." The
   template recognizes love only in the shape of pursuit.
4. A man goes silent for days after any criticism from his wife — not sulking; waiting, braced,
   for the ending where criticism was always the overture to being discarded.
5. A client brings a gift by session three and asks whether you have worse clients —
   auditioning for favorite, the way affection was earned at home.
6. A woman runs every friendship from the giving side; being cared for makes her itch. In her
   template, care flows one direction, and reversing it reads as danger.
7. In couples work, "I need more from you" lands on him as "you're failing" — every phrasing,
   every time. The template supplies the sentence's meaning before the words arrive.
8. A woman keeps her apartment as a museum no one visits and calls it "liking my space" —
   closeness at home was surveillance, so home is now where closeness isn't.
9. A man tests the therapist — cancels late, arrives late, watches what happens. The
   will-you-still-be-there experiment he has run on everyone who mattered.
10. Her partner raises a small complaint; she instantly concedes everything and goes small.
    Peace at any price, learned in a house where disagreement had a body count.

## In the machinery

Floor 4 is the furnace's home terrain: provoke and withdraw-effort operate almost exclusively
on relational predictions, because other people are the part of the world that can be recruited.
The gold loop spirals fastest here (each burned relationship files as independent evidence
about people). And at system scale, family-systems work is this floor with the loop distributed
across several bodies — each member manufacturing the others' predicted response.

## Check yourself

1. A client completes a full course of CBT for their relationship anxiety: beliefs revised,
   worksheets immaculate, pattern intact. Using the straddle, say precisely what was treated
   and what wasn't — and what the next treatment must contain.
2. Your own pull with a particular client is boredom, every session, unlike any other hour in
   your week. Formulate it as floor-4 data: what template might produce a bored therapist, and
   what would you test?
3. Why does the model insist the corrective relational experience must occur *while the
   template is live* rather than discussed after? Answer with the floor-6 write rules.
