# Floor 5 — Scripts and Behavior

Floor notes series, 12 August 2026. Companions: §3.1, §3.4, §3.5, §3.3 (shape the path), the
floor-presentations bank.

---

## The essence: the sequence that finishes first

The paper's definition: "learned sequences that run a familiar situation to its known end before
thought arrives." Floor 5 is the floor of *procedure without ceremony* — chains of action fired
by cues, executing start-to-finish below deliberation. The format is neither sentence nor story
nor raw feeling: it is **choreography**. The defining temporal fact: the script completes, or is
well underway, before the deliberate mind gets a vote — "by the time I notice, I've already
yelled" is not an excuse but an accurate report of the architecture (§3.3: the rider arrives
after the elephant has moved).

## Against its neighbors

- **Versus floor 3:** the person can hold the correct belief and run the contrary script — the
  two aren't even in the same format, so they don't collide. No amount of belief-revision
  rewrites choreography; only reps do.
- **Versus floor 6 — the crucial cut:** a script can run **cold**. Floor 5 sounds like *"I
  always end up…"* (habit, pattern, mild embarrassment); floor 6 sounds like *"it just hits
  me"* (weather, charge, bewilderment). Many sequences are hybrids — a script with a floor-6
  ignition — and the treatment differs by which component you're facing: reps and redesign for
  the cold sequence, activation-plus-mismatch for the hot ignition.
- **Versus floor 4:** template upstream, script downstream. The template interprets ("she's
  pulling away"); the script executes (the checking, the accusation). The furnace runs them in
  series, which is why interrupting the operant (§2.3) is a floor-5 intervention serving a
  floor-4 prior.

## How it gets written

By repetition under consequence — ordinary operant learning. Sequences that ended predictably
got compiled: the appeasement that shortened the fight, the joke that broke the tension, the
drink that ended the Sunday dread. Each was a solution once. Floor 5 is where solutions go to
outlive their problems.

## What reaches it — and why it works

§3.4 assigns **behavioral methods, exposure, and skills training**. The engine's account, via
§3.3's second lever: **shape the path** — don't argue with the sequence, change the terrain it
runs on. Concretely:

1. **Interrupt the cue → sequence link**: stimulus control, environment design, removing the
   trigger from reach — cheaper than willpower every time, because it spends no rider strength.
2. **Overwrite by repetition**: a competing sequence drilled until it compiles — skills
   training is literally writing a new floor-5 entry, and it needs the same thing the old one
   got: reps under realistic conditions.
3. **Exposure, when the script is avoidance**: the avoid move (§2.3) is a floor-5 sequence
   whose function is preventing the test; blocking it forces the reading the prior has been
   dodging. Response prevention keeps the room unheated while the reading is taken.

The dignity intervention comes free (§3.3): *knowing what to do and not doing it is the
ordinary architecture, not weakness of will* — saying so is often the first de-shaming move of
the treatment.

## From your chair

The §3.5 signs that point here: the client's own verbs ("I always end up…" sits in the
scripts); the same sequence every time the cue appears, visible to everyone but the person
running it; the sequence's *smoothness* — floor-5 material runs efficiently, without the
disproportion that marks floor 6.

The five presentations (composites):

1. "By the time I notice, I've already yelled." The sequence completes before thought arrives.
2. The Sunday-evening drink that pours itself — cue-fired, not decided.
3. "Every family dinner ends the same way. I walk in calm and it still happens." A situation
   that runs to its known end like a play.
4. The pre-emptive apologizer: sorry before anyone is upset — a learned sequence to a predicted
   ending.
5. The interview self-sabotage: the same deprecating joke at the same moment in every
   interview.

## Ten in the wild

All composites. The floor-5 tell in each: a *sequence* runs to its known end before thought
arrives — smooth, cue-fired, often nearly feelingless in the running.

1. A man opens the fridge every time he walks in the door. Not hungry — it's the coming-home
   sequence, installed at fourteen.
2. A woman meaning to drive to the farmer's market finds herself in the work parking lot on a
   Saturday. The commute ran itself.
3. His phone is in his hand with the app already open before he registers having picked it up.
   The boredom-to-scroll chain, fully compiled.
4. Every argument with his teenage son runs the identical five lines and ends with the same
   door slam. Both could write the transcript in advance; neither can exit it live.
5. A woman deflects every compliment with the same self-dig, same beat, same smile — a routine so smooth her friends could time it.
6. A man says "sorry" when someone bumps into *him*. The apology fires positionally, before
   fault is computed.
7. A woman cleans the kitchen the moment tension enters the house. Mop as choreography; her kids know exactly what a spotless counter means.
8. A man deploys the same joke about his baldness in the first two minutes of every meeting —
   a pre-emptive strike against an attack nobody was planning, on schedule.
9. Sunday, five o'clock: the wine glass is poured. Not decided — poured. The week's dread has a standing appointment.
10. A woman rewrites every text three times before sending — softening, adding warmth,
    removing directness. The smoothing pass runs on everything, including texts to her sister.

## In the machinery

Floor 5 is the furnace's motor pool: provoke, avoid, and withdraw-effort are all *executed* as
floor-5 sequences, whatever floor their employer-prior lives on. This is the formulation
two-step the model keeps insisting on: the script is rarely the problem; it is the problem's
*staff*. Treat the script alone (pure habit reversal) when it truly runs cold and ownerless;
expect it to regrow if a hot prior below is still issuing the work orders — the same reason the
transfer rule warns that conquering one riser's script leaves the others burning.

## Check yourself

1. A client wants to stop the Sunday drink. Design the floor-5 treatment (cue, terrain,
   competing sequence) — then name the finding that would tell you a floor-6 ignition is
   underneath and the plan must expand.
2. Why is exposure filed under floor 5's levers when its target is usually a floor-6 prior?
   Trace the full circuit: which floor hosts the block, which floor takes the write.
3. "It's not weakness of will; it's the architecture." Defend that sentence to a skeptical
   client using only the floors — no jargon.
