# Floor 6 — Procedure and Emotion

## The essence: fusion

The paper's definition — "stored action and sensory traces with built emotional responses fused
to them" — and the load-bearing word is **fused**. On floor 3, a belief and a feeling are two
things: you hold the sentence "dogs are dangerous," and fear arrives as a consequence of
consulting it. On floor 6 there is no consulting step. The dread is welded into the perception
itself — the dog-shaped fear arrives *as* the seeing, one unit, no assembly required. A floor-6
entry is a chunk of stored experience — an action tendency, a sensory signature, and an emotion —
cast as a single piece. In engine terms: a high-precision prediction stored in sensory-emotional
format, and because prediction is experience (§2.1), the prediction doesn't produce the feeling.
**The prediction is the feeling.**

## Against its neighbors

- **Versus floor 5 (scripts):** a script is a learned sequence that can run *cold* — the
  appeasement routine, the reflexive overcommit, driving to the old house on autopilot. It feels
  like habit. Floor 6 is when the sequence carries built-in weather: the emotion isn't a
  reaction to how the script turns out; it's inside the trace. The client tell: floor 5 sounds
  like *"I always end up…"*; floor 6 sounds like *"it just hits me."*
- **Versus floor 7 (body):** floor 7 is the alarm hardware itself — raw arousal, domain-general,
  the party line. The body alone cannot tell you which dial fired. Floor 6 is what decides that
  *this particular cue* is bell-worthy: it has content, a trigger signature, an original scene
  even when the scene is unrecallable. Tool choice follows: floor-7 work is **regulation**
  (works on any alarm); floor-6 work is **re-learning** (requires the specific fused trace).
- **The floor-4 straddle resolves here:** "relational patterns" names a *domain*; its deep
  component — the internal working model proper — is *stored in floor-6 format*. Floor 4 is
  what it's about; floor 6 is how it's kept. A riser passing through a floor.

## How it gets written

Emotional learning under stakes — sometimes one trial (the bite, the crash, the night everything
changed), sometimes ten thousand quiet ones (the particular shift in a parent's voice that
preceded the bad hour, learned before the words existed to describe it). Indexed by **sensory
signature**, not meaning: a tone, a smell, a quality of silence, footsteps of a certain weight.
This is why floor-6 reactions bewilder their owners — the trigger is a perceptual key, so the
reaction seems to come from nowhere.

## Why words bounce

Glass and concrete: a sentence is aimed at floor 3, and the floor-6 trace was never made of
sentences — there is nothing sentence-shaped for the argument to grip. The paper's freeze
example: complete, articulate understanding of why you freeze, and the freezing untouched. The
third furnace patient: reads the wall thermometer, agrees the room is 70, stays cold — the
wall's number is floor 3; the setting fires from 6. **Winning the argument and losing the
temperature is a floor reading**, and it points here more often than anywhere else.

## What reaches it — and why it works

The paper cites Ecker, Ticic & Hulley because the reconsolidation logic is the cleanest account
of floor-6 change, and every therapy that reaches this floor shares its shape:

1. **The trace must be live** — activated, felt in the room, not discussed from a distance. A
   dormant trace is closed to writing.
2. **A vivid mismatch while it's live** — an experience, not an argument, that contradicts what
   the trace predicts: the anger expressed at the empty chair and nothing terrible happening;
   the therapist met with the shameful thing and not flinching; the memory revisited while the
   body stays regulated.
3. **Repetition inside the window.**

EMDR, EFT, Gestalt enactment, imagery rescripting, the interpretation that lands in the heat of
the transference — different brands, one mechanism: *activate plus disconfirm, experientially.*
In engine words: no violation, no write — and a reading counts only at the precision it is
granted, which is why safety isn't a warm-up but half the intervention.

**The central paradox, held as a rule: floor 6 only updates while it's firing, and it can't
update while it's flooding.** You must visit the heat to change it — but past the window of
tolerance the valve slams shut and the elevator loses power, so nothing files. The craft of
every experiential therapy is holding the client in that band: activated enough to be writable,
regulated enough to write. Honesty flag the paper itself carries (§6.2): whether this is true
erasure or new learning outcompeting the old trace is contested — and the second can return,
which is why relapse is an expected outcome, not a refutation.

## From your chair

The §3.5 signs that point here: the disproportionate reaction with no story attached — too big,
and surprising to its own owner. The body answering first *with a specific trigger signature*.
Accurate insight that changes nothing. Tears before their reason — "I don't know why I'm crying"
is floor 6 opening in front of you; an invitation, not a malfunction. The reaction that finishes
before thought arrives *with charge* (the last clause separates it from a cold floor-5 script).

The five presentations (from the floor-presentations bank; all composites):

1. Rage at a tone of voice, tears at a smell — "I have no idea where that came from."
2. "I know he's safe. My body doesn't." Won on floor three, lost on floor six.
3. Flinches at a raised hand; can't sit with their back to a door — sensory-motor traces
   presenting as "anxiety."
4. The anniversary reaction: falls apart every October, has never connected the date.
5. Sexual shutdown with a loving partner — an old protection running against a present that no
   longer requires it.

## Ten in the wild

All composites — the fused trace firing: an emotion welded to a sensory key, arriving before
thought, bewildering its owner.

1. A grown man's stomach drops at the sound of keys in the front door at 6pm — his father's
   arrival time, thirty years ago. It's his gentle wife's keys now. The dread doesn't care.
2. A woman smells a particular aftershave in an elevator and is suddenly fighting tears with no
   idea why. She never consciously connected the scent to her grandfather's hospital room.
3. A combat veteran, years home, hits the grocery-store floor when a pallet drops — and is
   upright and embarrassed before his thinking mind has received the sound.
4. A man flinches, whole-body, when his wife raises her hand to fix her own hair in his
   peripheral vision. Nobody has raised a hand at him since he was nine.
5. Every late October a woman goes heavy, tearful, short-tempered — and is surprised every
   year. Her mother died in October. She has never connected the date; her body never lost it.
6. A man goes rigid the instant his boss shifts into a certain flat, controlled tone — not
   shouting, that specific *quiet*. That was the tone that meant the evening was about to go
   wrong.
7. A woman can't relax into her partner's touch though she chose him and trusts him — her body runs an old protection the moment hands reach for her. "I know he's safe. My body doesn't."
8. A man tears up, reliably and to his own embarrassment, at unexpected kindness — a waived
   fee, a stranger with jumper cables. Kindness touches something old that never got any, and
   the tears beat every thought to the scene.
9. A woman feels sudden, disproportionate rage when someone stands just behind her left
   shoulder while she works. Coworkers have learned. She has no story for it at all.
10. A man twenty years past his only crash still grips the door handle and stamps a phantom
    brake when his wife drives — and his explanation, "I'm just a bad passenger," is a floor-3
    label pasted over a floor-6 trace.

What makes all ten floor 6 rather than the neighbors: each has a *specific sensory key* — a
time, a smell, a sound, a tone, a position in space, a date, a kind of touch — which separates
them from floor 7's general, contentless alarm; and each carries *charge arriving fully
formed*, which separates them from a cold floor-5 habit. In most of the ten the owner has no
story or a wrong one — the trace fires below the floor where stories are kept, which is exactly
why insight alone leaves every one untouched.
