# Protocol — Social Anxiety, derived from the model

19 August 2026. Filed in Clinical Tools beside the Decision Aid, the Health Anxiety protocol, and
the Depression protocol.
**Honesty label, in the appendix spirit: made, not found.** This is a model-derived reorganization
of components that existing social anxiety treatments have tested separately — the Clark & Wells
cognitive model and its trials, the Rapee & Heimberg audience-representation account,
safety-behavior and attention-training work, video feedback, imagery rescripting, and the
inhibitory-learning exposure rules (Craske). The *sequence* is the model's contribution (gates →
policy → camera → armor → footage → ignition → consolidation → relapse plan), and the sequence has
no outcome data of its own. Written by a counseling student; runs under supervision; composes
with, never replaces, program training and site protocols. Companions: [[floor-notes/Floor 2 —
Metacognition]], [[floor-notes/Floor 5 — Scripts and Behavior]], [[floor-notes/Floor 3 —
Propositional Beliefs]], the furnace (§2.3), and the Salkovskis, Craske, Kube, and Swann pins in
the ledger.

## The formulation in one line

Social anxiety presents on floor 3 (the verdicts: defective, boring, visibly weak), runs its
engine on floors 5 and 2 (the armor loop, governed by an inward-facing camera and a post-mortem
policy), is usually ignited from floor 6 (a humiliation scene, a critical house), sits in floor-8
water (worth priced in performance and likability), and is worn on the rank dial — and the
treatment follows the building, not the complaint.

## The model's distinctive reading

Three claims organize everything below.

**Social anxiety is a rank forecast mistaken for a rank measurement.** The felt certainty in the
doorway — *they can all see it, they're already judging* — arrives with the authority of
perception, and it is not perception. It is the machine's render of the room (§2.1), drawn from a
prior about what audiences do to people like this, delivered so early and so vividly that it
replaces the room. The rank dial is standard human equipment — status tracking was survival
accounting for a group species, and everyone runs it — but this dial's precision is cranked to
hair-trigger and its deadband is set to zero: every micro-signal (a glance, a pause, a flat
"okay") trips a full status alarm. The client is not misreading the audience. The client is
reading the forecast *instead of* the audience.

**The camera faces inward.** At the exact moments when audience data would disconfirm the
forecast, attention flips to self-monitoring — the heartbeat, the heat in the face, the sound of
the own voice — and the machine constructs the audience's view out of interoception: felt warmth
becomes visible scarlet, a felt tremble becomes a seen shake, the mind gone blank becomes a
silence everyone is timing. That constructed self-portrait, usually rendered from the observer's
perspective, gets filed as *observation*. This is the model's sharpest claim about the condition:
the client has decades of social experience and almost no audience data — the camera was pointed
the wrong way the entire time.

**The armor manufactures the evidence.** Safety behaviors — rehearsing every sentence, gripping
the glass, hiding the hands, safe topics only, low voice, early exit, eyes on the floor — run the
same fraudulent accounting as depression's withdraw-effort move: every survived room is logged as
*saved by the armor*, so the catastrophe compiles an undefeated record without ever taking the
field. And here the loop has a second, crueler gear: the armor partially *causes* the impression
it guards against — the rehearsed answer reads as stiff, the low voice as disinterest, the averted
eyes as coldness — so the machine's forecast manufactures a little of its own confirming data. The
other furnace moves run guard duty in social costume: *avoid* (the declined invitation, the unsent
message), *filter* (the friendliness discounted — "they were just being polite"), *reinterpret*
(the good evening immunized — "they didn't notice this time"; Kube, pinned), and *provoke*
(self-deprecation fishing for reassurance, the confirming audiences sought out — Swann's
self-verification, pinned). Treatment is therefore furnace work with the camera turned first:
footage, then experiments — argument alone gets immunized.

## Phase 0 — The gates (run first, and never fully retired)

**Mood and risk.** Social anxiety travels with depression often enough that the depression gates
run here too, in reduced form: PHQ-9 alongside the LSAS at baseline, item-9 follow-up per the site
protocol, and the full [[Protocol — Depression]] Phase 0 the moment the profile darkens. Long
avoidance histories carry real losses — friendships unmade, careers capped — and the grief in that
is named, not treated as distortion.

**The alcohol gate.** Drink is the most common self-prescription for this condition, and the
protocol's rule is stated early and without moralizing: experiments run sober or they don't count.
Alcohol is liquid armor — whatever happens with it on board gets credited to it, and the learning
doesn't transfer to the sober body (state-dependent, and the machine knows it). Screen (AUDIT-C or
equivalent); where the drinking has its own engine, sequence or refer per supervision.

**The differential, not the exclusion: a genuinely different machine.** Where the profile suggests
autistic social processing — lifelong, context-independent, present in safe rooms too — the
protocol's central claim (*skill present, inhibited by forecast*) may be partly false, and running
disconfirmation experiments against accurate self-knowledge is invalidation, not treatment. The
sorting question is whether the difficulty is *pricing* (skills that appear intact with trusted
people and vanish under evaluation → this protocol) or *processing* (a different operating style
across all rooms → adapt: treat the catastrophic pricing and the post-mortem, leave the accurate
self-knowledge alone, and consider whether an affirming frame or referral serves better).

**The accurate-forecast gate.** Sometimes the room really is hostile — the bullying workplace, the
harassing classmate, the family that mocks, the room where the client's body or accent or identity
draws real contempt. A dial reading a hostile room correctly is not a disorder. Ecology first:
safety, exits, allies, reporting channels where relevant. Experiments get run in fair rooms; the
skill being trained is telling fair rooms from rigged ones, and the protocol never gaslights a
correct forecast.

**The visible-symptom calibration.** Where there is a real, visible symptom — hyperhidrosis, an
essential tremor, a stutter, hard blushing — the target shifts and says so honestly: not *it
doesn't show* (it may) but *what the audience actually does about it* — which the machine has
forecast as contempt and never once measured. Experiments price the verdict, not the symptom.
Medical referral runs alongside where the symptom is treatable and the client wants it treated.

**The medication boundary.** SSRIs are legitimate first-line company for this protocol — alongside,
not instead, and nothing here outranks the prescriber. The specific flag this condition adds: PRN
benzodiazepines and pre-event beta-blockers can function as chemical armor — the survived
presentation gets credited to the pill, and benzodiazepines may blunt the very learning the
experiments exist to produce. That observation goes to the prescriber as a scheduling question,
never to the client as an instruction.

## Phase 1 — Locate and formulate (the riser profile)

Two sessions of ordinary listening, Appendix A machinery with a rank filter. The sorting
questions:

- *"Who was the first audience?"* The critical parent, the classroom that laughed, the sibling who
  mocked, the move to the school where everything was wrong — floor 6 usually holds a scene or a
  house, and the profile wants it located even though Phase 6 may never need to visit.
- *"What exactly would they see?"* Not "I'd be anxious" — the specific feared exhibit: the blush,
  the blank, the boring, the weird, the trying-too-hard. This sentence is the forecast, verbatim,
  and it becomes the named opponent of Phases 4 and 5.
- *"What do you do to make sure it doesn't show?"* The armor inventory, itemized and written:
  rehearsal, scripts, props, posture, camouflage, exits, the questions asked to keep the spotlight
  moving. Phase 4 needs this list; clients are usually startled by its length, and the startle is
  clinical data.
- *"And afterward — how long does the trial run?"* Surfaces the post-mortem: the replay, the
  evidence combed for the wince, the verdict filed at 2 a.m. Floor 2's policy, named in the
  client's own vocabulary.

Note the specifier: a performance-only presentation (the talk, the toast, the recital — fine at
dinner) is a narrower riser, often with floor-8 stakes and no floor-6 scene; the protocol shrinks
to fit rather than assuming the whole building is involved.

Baseline in numbers, because the numbers are the outcome measures: LSAS, PHQ-9, the avoidance
list, the armor inventory count, a week of social-contact sampling (what was declined, attended,
endured, enjoyed), plus the client's own three-item list of what a lifted month would contain.

## Phase 2 — Teach the client the machine

Client-facing telling, in the model's images, and the dignity move comes standard: **nothing here
is broken.** The rank dial is ancient, universal equipment — for a group species, standing was
food, protection, mating, survival, and a machine that tracked it hard kept its owner alive. This
machine learned, usually in a real house or a real cafeteria, that audiences are graders and
grades are dangerous — and it has been protecting its owner from that classroom ever since, in
every room, at maximum precision. Its logic is coherent; its *forecast* is wrong about these
rooms. Then the claims that reorganize everything, stated flat:

*The judgment you feel is a forecast, not a measurement.* And: *you have almost no actual footage
of any audience — the camera has been pointed at you.*

Name the prepayment while it's on the table: the two weeks of dread before the wedding *is* the
machine charging admission in advance — the event gets paid for many times before it happens, and
the price is counted as evidence of how bad it must be.

## Phase 3 — Floor 2 first, because floor 2 is the gatekeeper

In this riser the gatekeeper runs two operations, and both get retrained before the armor comes
off — experiments run with the camera still inward just generate more renders.

**Turn the camera (attention).** The discovery experiment, in session and then live: two short
conversations, one with attention locked on self (monitor everything — the machine's default), one
with attention deliberately out (their words, their face, the actual room), then compare anxiety,
performance, and — the punchline — *how much audience data came back*. The client discovers
they've been reading themselves for years and calling it the audience. Then situational attention
training as a rep: out the door, attention out, every social entry a camera drill. This is not
distraction and the difference is stated: distraction avoids data; the turned camera *collects*
it.

![[social-anxiety-camera.png]]

*The discovery experiment, drawn — camera in versus camera out.*

**Close the courtroom (the post-mortem policy).** The post-event trial is the gatekeeper's
conviction that replaying the evening long enough will either fix it or at least price the damage
accurately — cost-account it against its record (has the 2 a.m. retrial ever once returned a
kinder verdict, or a useful one?). The replacement, not the ban: one structured reading, same
night or next morning — *what did I predict, what did I actually observe, what surprised me* —
written, two minutes, done. Anything after that is circling, and gets caught and named, not
suppressed.

Deliverable: consent to the experiment. "We're testing the machine's forecasts about rooms. Your
worth was never on the table."

## Phase 4 — Interrupt the furnace (floor 5): the armor comes off

The chassis is the two-condition experiment (Wells and Clark's classic, run in the house format):
the same social task twice, armor on versus armor off, with a written forecast *before* each
(anxiety 0–10, "how badly it will show" 0–10, the machine's exact prediction in words) and a
written reading *after*. The robust finding the client collects personally: armor off costs more
for the first minutes and *reads better* — warmer, more present, more competent — and the anxiety
peaks and falls faster without the monitoring. The gap between forecast and reading, in their own
handwriting, is the treatment — evidence laid at the exact address where the code lives. Where a
recording is possible, Phase 5 pairs it with footage.

Design rules, per Craske and the ledger: expectancy violation over habituation — each experiment
is built to test a *stated* prediction, sized so the machine actually shows up (comfortable
experiments teach nothing), varied across rooms and armor items rather than climbed like a ladder,
and run to the read-out, not to the anxiety's exit. The furnace's counterattack is named in
advance, move by move, so the client can catch it: the *filter* audited weekly (what warmth
arrived that never made it into memory?), the *reinterpret* logged next to the reading it attacks
("they didn't notice this time" gets written down and dated, so its record can be checked), the
*provoke* caught live (the self-deprecation that fishes, the "was that okay?" that solicits — 
Joiner's reassurance-seeking, pinned, is this move's social form), the quiet *avoid* and
*withdraw-effort* (attending in body while participation stays home — present at the party,
absent from it) tracked in the contact sampling. **Session metric (§6.2): if nothing surprised the
client, nothing changed** — an experiment that merely confirmed expectations was sized wrong;
resize and rerun.

Graduation, when the armor experiments have their legs: test the core forecast directly.
Deliberate, small, visible imperfection — the obvious question asked, the mild disagreement filed,
the mediocre joke told, the pause taken without apology, the keys dropped — against the machine's
flat prediction that visible flaw equals status collapse. What the readings return, almost every
time: the audience's deadband is enormous. People barely register, forget in minutes, and warm to
the human one. The client's dial was calibrated for a courtroom; the world is mostly a bus stop.

## Phase 5 — Supply the readings (footage and the dimmer)

With the camera turned and the armor loosening, feed the starved instruments actual data:

- **Footage, run properly.** Video (or audio) feedback is the single most direct correction this
  condition allows — clients systematically overestimate how visible the anxiety is, and the tape
  disagrees with the render. The procedure matters: predict first, in operational terms ("the
  shake will be visible the whole time," "the pause will last five seconds"), brief the client to
  watch as a stranger would (not hunting the felt moments — the felt version is the render under
  review), then compare tape to forecast, line by line. Where video is infeasible: trusted-observer
  ratings, the therapist's own structured read-back, audio for the voice the client is sure
  everyone hears breaking.
- **The visibility experiments.** For the blush, the sweat, the tremble: measure it. Confederates
  where the setting allows, honest co-experimenters otherwise ("ask three people afterward what
  they noticed"). The machine has been pricing a symptom no one has ever actually surveyed.
- **The dimmer is the covert experiment.** The therapy relationship runs its quiet disconfirmation
  here as everywhere: an audience that sees the blank, the blush, and the fumble, week after week,
  and doesn't grade. Watch for the furnace eating it — "you're paid to be nice" is this riser's
  version of the depression protocol's move, and it gets named gently and then *used*: an honest
  read-back from a person with no stake in politeness is footage too. Where a group format exists,
  it is this phase's natural amplifier — eight live audiences, all collecting data.

## Phase 6 — The ignition scene, if Phase 1 found one

Where a floor-6 trace is underneath — the read-aloud that went wrong, the cafeteria, the parent's
public corrections, the year of being the new kid — the experiments lift the rooms of the present
and leave a pilot light burning in that one. The tell: Phases 3–5 restore function, but a dateless
flinch persists, or one class of trigger stays hot (authority, laughter, being watched writing),
or the encapsulated sentence surfaces intact — *I am the one who gets laughed at.* The trace
needs activation-plus-mismatch: the scene revisited while regulated, the meaning re-metabolized
with the adult's data in the room (imagery rescripting lineage — Hackmann, Wild, Clark), within
scope and supervision, or by referral until licensure. Updating one scene here routinely moves
rooms Phases 4–5 never touched — the model's account of why: the rooms were all being priced by
that one classroom.

## Phase 7 — Consolidation up the building

Now floor 3 writes, because footage exists for the sentences to describe: the client states the
revised model in their own words, against their own ledger of forecasts-versus-readings — never
the therapist's testimony, which this machine files under politeness. Floor 1 re-authors: the
person whose story was *the awkward one* becomes *the one whose machine priced every room as a
courtroom, and who walked back in and read the actual verdicts* — made, not found, and said out
loud. Where floor-8 water is in play — worth priced in performance, likability as solvency, the
imagined jury staffed by the culture's graders — it gets named and renegotiated explicitly, or
the riser rebuilds with the next promotion, wedding season, or feed refresh. And the rank dial
gets its legal channels listed and scheduled — real competence built where it matters to them,
opinions filed in public at survivable stakes, the places they're counted on — because a dial
this sensitive goes quiet when it's fed, not when it's lectured.

## Phase 8 — Relapse as forecast, not failure

Real rooms occasionally go badly — the joke lands wrong, the interviewer is cold, the rejection is
actual — and the model expects the machine to claim total vindication and says so in advance. The
ending includes a written **flare protocol**: size the event honestly (one room, one evening, one
audience — an n of 1 laid against the whole ledger, and grief allowed where something real was
lost); the fast re-entry rule — one ordinary experiment inside the week, before avoidance
re-compounds, because the machine consolidates its win in the gap; and the client's personal
early-warning signature, named while well (the declines creeping back "just this once," rehearsal
returning before calls, the post-mortems lengthening, the phone screened). Booster sessions are
named as maintenance, not failure. Close by reviewing the baseline numbers — the LSAS, the
contact sampling, the armor count — as the story of the treatment.

## Two therapist-side rules, all phases

1. **Never argue with the verdict.** Reassurance is doubly poisoned in this riser, because the
   therapist *is* an audience: "you seemed fine to me" gets filed under paid politeness, and each
   defeated argument logs as proof that even the professional case was weak. The answer is always
   footage and experiments, never testimony. And watch the room itself: therapy can become the one
   audience the client doesn't avoid — the safest armor yet. The session exists to route data in
   from the world, not to substitute for it.
2. **Match the tempo, and watch the transfer rule.** All the armor off at once is flooding with
   better branding — the removal is graded even though the framing is honest about the
   destination. And conquering this riser doesn't touch the others: expect the same machinery in
   new costumes — dating after work rooms are won, authority after peers, the pen that still
   freezes when someone watches the signature — and expect the sibling risers from the same house
   (perfectionism, over-preparation, the workhorse who can't be seen resting), and say so before
   they report in.
